      The 5 golden rules

      1. Act fast — fresh stains are 10× easier.
      2. Blot from the outside in. Never rub.
      3. Cold water first for protein (blood, milk, egg, sweat).
      4. No heat (dryer, iron, hot water) until the stain is gone.
      5. Never mix bleach with ammonia, vinegar or other cleaners.

      Why stains behave differently

      Most household stains fall into a handful of chemical families, and the family decides the treatment. Protein stains (blood, egg, dairy, sweat, vomit, urine) bond to fibres when heated, so they need cold water and enzyme detergents. Tannin stains (coffee, tea, wine, juice, tomato) are plant-based dyes that respond to mild acids, detergent and oxygen bleach. Oil-based stains (grease, butter, makeup) repel water — they need an absorbent like cornstarch first and a surfactant such as dish soap. Dye stains (ink, grass, turmeric, marker) usually need a solvent like rubbing alcohol. Particulate stains (mud, wax, gum) are physical — dry, freeze or scrape before you wet anything.

      Knowing the family means you can tackle stains that aren't on this list. Identify what the stain is made of, pick the matching approach, and work gently from the least aggressive method upward.

      Your basic stain kit

      • White microfibre cloths and paper towels
      • Clear dish soap and an enzyme laundry detergent
      • Oxygen bleach (sodium percarbonate)
      • 3% hydrogen peroxide (for whites and light colours only)
      • Rubbing alcohol (isopropyl, 70%)
      • White vinegar and baking soda (used separately)
      • A soft brush and a plastic scraper
      Safety: Work in a ventilated room, wear gloves, keep products in original labelled containers, and never combine chlorine bleach with ammonia, vinegar or other acids.
