Carpets trap dust, allergens and soil deep in their fibres. Good vacuuming does most of the work; prompt spot treatment and an occasional deep clean do the rest.

Vacuum like you mean it

  • Frequency: high-traffic areas twice a week (daily with pets), the rest weekly.
  • Technique: move slowly — one pass forward and one back at half speed — and overlap rows. Change direction occasionally to lift pile.
  • Equipment: a vacuum with a brush roll for carpet, adjusted to the right height, and a sealed HEPA filter for allergy households. Empty the bin or bag when half full; suction drops as it fills.

Spot treatment

Act fast, blot from the outside in, and use the least liquid that works — overwetting soaks the pad and can cause odours or mould. A simple solution of a teaspoon of clear dish soap in a cup of warm water handles most food spills. Protein stains like blood need cold water; pet urine needs an enzyme cleaner. Deep cleaning: DIY extractor or professional?

Most manufacturers recommend hot-water extraction (often called steam cleaning) every 12–18 months, more often with pets or children. You can rent an extractor from many supermarkets and hardware stores; it's cost-effective for a few rooms if you have the time.

  1. Move furniture and vacuum thoroughly — extraction is not a substitute for dry soil removal.
  2. Pre-treat traffic lanes and stains with the rental company's pre-spray.
  3. Use the recommended amount of solution (more soap = sticky residue that attracts dirt).
  4. Make one wet pass and two or three dry (suction-only) passes.
  5. Ventilate and run fans; aim to be dry within 6–12 hours. Put foil or plastic tabs under furniture legs if you move them back early.

Professionals use truck-mounted equipment with more heat and suction, which dries faster and removes more soil. In 2026 prices typically run about $35–$75 per room, with stain and pet treatment extra. Check your carpet warranty — some require professional cleaning.

Odour control

Sprinkle baking soda, leave for an hour (or overnight), then vacuum thoroughly. Persistent pet odours usually mean urine has reached the pad — enzyme treatment that saturates the area, or professional sub-surface extraction, is needed.

Make carpets last longer

  • Doormats outside and inside every entrance
  • Shoes-off policy
  • Rugs or runners in traffic lanes
  • Rearrange furniture occasionally to change wear patterns
  • Close blinds during peak sun to reduce fading
