A two-hour cleanup with a dozen neighbours can fill dozens of bags and change how a whole street treats its public spaces. The difference between a chaotic event and a great one is about a week of planning.

Four weeks out: pick a site and get permission

  • Choose a site you can realistically clear in 2–3 hours, with a safe meeting point, toilets nearby and no heavy traffic.
  • Contact the land manager — usually your city or council parks department, a conservation authority for rivers and beaches, or the property owner. Many councils lend equipment and collect bags for free if you register.
  • Ask about hazards they know of (needles, unstable banks, tides) and where to leave full bags.

Three weeks out: recruit

  • Register your cleanup on Clean.World and list it publicly.
  • Post in neighbourhood groups, schools, places of worship, local businesses and sports clubs. Corporate volunteer days are an easy way to reach 20+ people at once.
  • Use a sign-up form to collect names, contact details and emergency contacts, and to get waivers if your land manager requires them.
  • Ask a local business to sponsor gloves, bags, water or snacks — they get a shout-out, you get supplies.

One week out: supplies and safety plan

  • ☐ Thick gloves and litter pickers (one per person)
  • ☐ Two bag colours: general waste and recycling
  • ☐ Hi-vis vests if near roads
  • ☐ First-aid kit and someone who knows how to use it
  • ☐ Sharps container, or a plan to mark and report sharps to the council
  • ☐ Water, sunscreen, hand sanitiser
  • ☐ Bathroom scale or luggage scale to weigh bags
  • ☐ Data sheet or app to tally item types

On the day

  1. Safety briefing (5 minutes). Boundaries of the site, no touching needles or broken glass by hand, no entering water or climbing banks, stay in pairs, children supervised by their own adults at all times, report anything suspicious.
  2. Split into teams with a team lead each and a defined zone.
  3. Collect and sort into waste and recycling as you go.
  4. Weigh and count before bags go to the collection point.
  5. Photos: before, during and after — with consent from anyone identifiable, and no photos of minors without guardian permission.
  6. Thank everyone and share the total on the spot.

After the event

Confirm bag collection, send a thank-you message with results and photos, and report your kilograms to add them to the global Clean.World counter. Share what you found: data about the most common items (cigarette butts, bottle caps, food wrappers) is powerful evidence when asking councils for more bins or businesses to change packaging. Then enter your crew in the monthly Cleanup Crew contest.

Keep it going: regular events — monthly or seasonally at the same site — build community and make a measurable difference over time. Recurring events are also easier to sponsor.
