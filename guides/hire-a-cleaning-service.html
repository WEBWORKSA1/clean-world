Hiring a cleaner can buy back hours every week — but price, quality and trust vary widely. Here's how to compare providers like a pro and avoid expensive surprises.

Company, independent cleaner or platform?

  • Cleaning companies usually carry insurance, bonding and backup staff if your regular cleaner is sick — and cost more.
  • Independent cleaners are often cheaper and more personal, but you may need to check insurance yourself and, in some countries, consider employment and tax rules.
  • Marketplaces and quote services (like Clean.World's free quotes) save time by letting several providers compete for the same job.

Questions to ask every provider

  1. Are you insured (general liability) and bonded? Can you send proof?
  2. Do you run background checks on staff?
  3. What exactly is included in a standard clean — and what costs extra (inside oven/fridge, windows, laundry)?
  4. Do you bring supplies and equipment? Can you use products I provide, or eco-friendly ones?
  5. Will the same cleaner come each time?
  6. What happens if I'm not satisfied — do you re-clean for free?
  7. What's your cancellation and lockout policy?
  8. How do you handle breakages?

Comparing quotes fairly

Make sure each quote covers the same scope. A cheap hourly rate with a vague estimate can cost more than a higher flat rate. Ask for an itemised checklist, the estimated hours and team size, and whether the first visit is priced as a deep clean (many companies require one before recurring service). Red flags

  • No proof of insurance, or reluctance to provide it
  • Cash-only with no written agreement or receipt
  • Prices far below everyone else's (often means rushed work or uninsured labour)
  • Upfront payment for multiple visits before the first clean
  • Pressure to decide immediately, or extra fees that appear only on the day
  • No reviews, or reviews that all appeared in the same week

Getting the best results

Declutter before visits so cleaners spend time cleaning rather than tidying. Write a short priority list for each visit, give feedback early and specifically, and treat your cleaner with respect — fair pay and clear expectations lead to the best long-term service. Tipping customs vary; in the US 10–20% for one-off jobs or a holiday bonus for recurring service is common.
