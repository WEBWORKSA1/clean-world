Most bathroom grime comes from three things: soap scum (fatty residue), limescale (mineral deposits from hard water) and mildew (moisture). Each needs a different approach — which is why one "bathroom spray" rarely does it all.

Know your enemy

  • Soap scum is greasy and dull-white. Alkaline cleaners and degreasers, or a dish-soap-and-baking-soda scrub, break it down.
  • Limescale is hard and chalky, often around taps and on shower glass. Acids dissolve it: white vinegar, citric acid or a commercial descaler — but never on natural stone.
  • Mildew appears as black or pink spots in grout and silicone. It needs a mould-specific product and, above all, drying and ventilation.

Step-by-step

  1. Ventilate and pre-spray. Turn on the fan, open a window, and spray the shower and tub with your cleaner so it can dwell while you do other jobs.
  2. Toilet. Apply toilet cleaner under the rim and let it sit. Wipe the exterior top to bottom: tank, handle, seat (both sides), hinges, base and the floor around it. Then scrub the bowl and flush.
  3. Shower glass. For limescale, wipe with equal parts warm vinegar and water, leave 10–15 minutes, scrub with a non-scratch pad and rinse. Squeegee dry.
  4. Tiles and tub. Scrub soap scum with a soft scrub (baking soda + dish soap) or your bathroom cleaner. Rinse well.
  5. Grout. Scrub with a baking-soda paste and a grout brush. For deeper stains on cement grout, oxygen bleach mixed with warm water and left for 15 minutes works well. Rinse. Consider sealing grout once it's fully dry.
  6. Silicone mildew. Apply a mould-removing gel (or a bleach-soaked paper towel strip on white silicone) and leave as the label directs, with ventilation. If black spots remain deep in the silicone, the only lasting fix is to cut it out and re-caulk.
  7. Taps and showerhead. Tie a bag of vinegar around the showerhead for an hour (not on brass, nickel or gold finishes — check your manufacturer's advice). Wipe taps and polish dry.
  8. Sink, mirror, surfaces, floor. Finish top to bottom and mop last, including behind the toilet.
Never mix toilet cleaner, bleach and vinegar. Many toilet bowl cleaners are acidic; combined with bleach they release chlorine gas. Flush and rinse between products.

Keep it clean with 2 minutes a day

  • Squeegee shower walls and glass after every shower — this alone prevents most scale and mildew.
  • Leave the shower door or curtain open to dry.
  • Run the fan for 20–30 minutes after bathing.
  • Wipe the sink and tap with the hand towel before it goes in the laundry.

Hard-water areas build scale fast. A water softener or a showerhead filter reduces it significantly; check your local water hardness report.
