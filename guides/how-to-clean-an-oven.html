A greasy oven isn't just unpleasant — burnt-on residue smokes, smells and transfers flavours to food. The good news: with a baking-soda paste and some patience, most ovens come clean without harsh fumes or hours of scrubbing.

What you'll need

  • ½ cup baking soda and a few tablespoons of water
  • White vinegar in a spray bottle
  • Rubber gloves, a plastic scraper or old card, and a non-scratch scrub sponge
  • Microfibre cloths and a bucket of warm water
  • An old towel or newspaper for the floor

Method 1: the baking-soda overnight clean

This is the lowest-fume method and works on most enamel oven interiors. It relies on time rather than force: alkaline baking soda slowly softens polymerised grease so it can be lifted rather than ground off.

  1. Prepare the oven. Make sure it is off and completely cool. Remove racks, thermometers and pizza stones. Lay a towel in front of the oven to catch drips.
  2. Mix the paste. Combine ½ cup baking soda with 2–3 tablespoons of water until it is spreadable, like frosting. Make a second batch for large ovens.
  3. Coat the interior. Wearing gloves, spread paste over the base, sides, back and the inside of the door — avoid heating elements, the fan and gas outlets. Pack it thicker on dark, crusty patches. It will turn brownish; that's normal.
  4. Wait 12 hours. Close the door and leave it overnight. This contact time does the work.
  5. Scrape and wipe. Use a plastic scraper to lift the softened paste and grime, then wipe with a damp cloth. Rinse the cloth often.
  6. Vinegar rinse. Lightly spray remaining white residue with vinegar. It will fizz as it reacts with the leftover baking soda — wipe it away, then do a final wipe with clean water.
  7. Dry and reassemble. Run the oven at a low temperature for 10 minutes to dry it before replacing the racks.

Cleaning the racks

Racks are often dirtier than the oven itself. The easiest approach is a long soak. Place racks in a bathtub lined with an old towel (to protect the enamel), cover with hot water and add ½ cup of dishwasher detergent or a generous squeeze of dish soap. Leave them for several hours or overnight, then scrub with a non-scratch brush and rinse. For extremely baked-on racks, sprinkle baking soda first, then spray vinegar before adding the water. Avoid steel wool on chrome-plated racks — it scratches the finish and encourages rust.

The door glass

Treat the inside of the glass with baking-soda paste for 20–30 minutes, then lift with a razor-blade scraper held at a low angle (glass only, never on enamel). For grime between the panes, check your manual: most modern doors either have a removable inner pane or can be partly disassembled by removing a few screws along the top. If your door cannot be opened, a thin cloth wrapped around a long flat tool and slid through the vent slots at the bottom can reach drips.

Self-cleaning and steam-clean ovens

Self-cleaning (pyrolytic) ovens heat to around 430–480°C (800–900°F) to burn residue to ash. They work, but they produce smoke and odour, and the extreme heat can occasionally stress components. Remove racks unless your manual says they are pyrolytic-safe, wipe away loose debris first, ventilate well, and keep pet birds out of the home entirely — fumes from overheated non-stick coatings can be fatal to birds. Never use commercial oven cleaner or baking-soda paste inside a pyrolytic oven; residues can damage the coating.

Steam-clean functions use water in the base to loosen light soil. They are great for regular maintenance but won't shift heavy, long-term buildup — combine them with the baking-soda method twice a year.

Using commercial oven cleaner safely

Heavy-duty oven sprays usually contain sodium hydroxide (lye). They are fast, but caustic. If you use one: ventilate the room, wear gloves and eye protection, keep children and pets away, protect the floor and any aluminium or painted parts, and follow the label exactly. Never mix oven cleaner with other products, and rinse thoroughly so no residue heats up the next time you cook. "Fume-free" formulations are less irritating but still require gloves.

Safety: Never use oven cleaner on a hot oven unless the label specifically says so, never spray it on heating elements or the fan, and keep it far away from gas pilot lights.

Keeping it clean

  • Wipe spills as soon as the oven has cooled — a fresh spill takes seconds, a baked one takes hours.
  • Place a sheet pan on the rack below juicy roasts and bubbling casseroles (don't line the oven floor with foil; it can block airflow and damage the finish).
  • Do a quick baking-soda spot treatment once a month and a full clean every three to six months, depending on how often you cook.
