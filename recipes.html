🍋 Green cleaning

DIY cleaner recipes that actually work

A handful of cheap ingredients replaces most of the spray bottles under your sink — as long as you know what each one can and can't do.

Never mix: bleach + vinegar (chlorine gas) · bleach + ammonia (chloramine gas) · bleach + rubbing alcohol (chloroform and other toxic compounds) · hydrogen peroxide + vinegar in the same bottle (peracetic acid, corrosive) · different drain cleaners. Rinse a surface with water before switching products, and keep every mix in a clearly labelled bottle.

🪟 Streak-free glass spray

  • 1 cup water (distilled avoids spots)
  • 1 cup white vinegar
  • 1 tbsp rubbing alcohol (70%)

Spray, wipe with a flat-weave microfibre, buff dry. Keep away from natural stone.

🧽 All-purpose cleaner

  • 2 cups warm water
  • ½ tsp clear dish soap
  • Optional: 10 drops lemon or tea-tree oil

Safe on sealed counters, painted walls, appliance fronts and most surfaces — including stone.

✨ Soft scrub for sinks & tubs

  • ½ cup baking soda
  • Enough dish soap to make a frosting-like paste

Scrub with a non-scratch sponge; rinse well. Great on bathtub rings and stovetops.

🪵 Hardwood & laminate floor wash

  • 4 litres / 1 gallon warm water
  • 1 tsp pH-neutral dish soap

Use a well-wrung mop — standing water damages wood. Skip vinegar on waxed or oiled floors.

🚿 Shower & kettle descaler

  • Equal parts white vinegar and water

Soak showerheads in a bag for an hour; boil a kettle half-full, leave 30 minutes, rinse twice.

🧺 Laundry whitening soak

  • 4 litres warm water
  • 2 tbsp oxygen bleach (sodium percarbonate)

Soak whites 1–6 hours, then wash. Not for silk, wool or leather.

Where DIY falls short

Vinegar and baking soda are excellent at what they do — dissolving mineral scale and gently scouring — but they are not registered disinfectants. For raw meat juices, illness in the home or bathroom surfaces after stomach bugs, use a product registered as a disinfectant in your country and follow its label contact time. Mixing baking soda and vinegar together mostly produces salty water and a satisfying fizz; use them separately for real cleaning power.

Avoid vinegar, lemon and other acids on marble, limestone, travertine, unsealed grout and waxed wood — they etch and dull the surface. Essential oils can be harmful to cats and birds; skip them in homes with those pets.

Buying green products

If you prefer ready-made products, look for independent certifications rather than vague words like "natural" or "eco". Examples include the US EPA Safer Choice label, EU Ecolabel, Nordic Swan and similar schemes in your region. Concentrates and refills in reusable bottles cut both cost and plastic waste.
